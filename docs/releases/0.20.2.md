# PhotoStore 0.20.2 — preserve original preview cadence

**Status: cadence boundary check passed; production build and delivery pending.**

Inspection of 0.20.1's production frame-hash counts revealed that its 180 requests presented only
120 distinct frames from the 30 fps synthetic fixture. Direct full-frame checks confirmed the
sequence `0,1,1,3,4,4,…`, while original decoding produced every frame. The optimization improved
latency but did not establish complete playback cadence.

FFmpeg first rescales source PTS to the cache's nanosecond clock, then rounds upward to the output
frame-rate grid to implement held-frame selection. Source timestamps such as 2/30 s can round just
above their exact rational value. Upward rounding then places them in the following output slot.
A one-tick bias before upward rounding absorbs that nanosecond rescale error. Sequential and bounded
preview windows share the correction. This preserves held-frame behavior outside the clock's
nanosecond quantization boundary; it does not switch to half-frame/nearest sampling.

The additional narrow regression passed, comparing every decoded frame against original indexed decoding at
30 and 30000/1001 fps, after start/middle/tail seeks, plus VFR gaps, a genuinely earlier target, and
the bounded-window sampler. The final production measurement must also retain all 180 distinct
fixture frame hashes. Native GPU/display/input/audio acceptance remains pending in #23.

All 0.20.1 scalability work is retained: shared pixel tiles/frame banks, persistent bounded decode,
cheap readiness, playback-aware thumbnail scheduling, adaptive derived media and configured-budget
background/atomic timeline operations replacing fixed 64 MiB authoring rejections. The original
source/export, editable Smart and v3 serialization contracts remain. Distinct recording, codec,
load and Smart transform bounds are recorded in [0.20.1](0.20.1.md).
